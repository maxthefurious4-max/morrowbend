Illustrative control-flow demo / synthetic data

A workflow should show its work.

This scripted front-end simulation turns a deliberately messy client email into a structured brief, asks only for missing information, waits for human approval, and records the trace. No customer data and no invented case study.

01 / Raw request

The input is incomplete on purpose.

FROM: maya@northstar.example
ACCOUNT: Northstar Studio
SUBJECT: October launch — need help quickly
ATTACHMENT: notes-v3.docx

Hi team—We need the campaign ready by October 14, though Sam mentioned the 18th might still be okay. It should cover the new advisory service and probably target existing clients first.

Could you get a kickoff moving? The old deck has most of the positioning. I’m not sure who needs to sign off now that Lena is out. We should keep the spend reasonable.

Thanks,
Maya Chen

Known traps: two dates, no budget, missing approver, vague audience, attachment not treated as ground truth.

Draft / not sent

Hi Maya—Thanks, I can get the kickoff prepared. Before we lock the brief, could you confirm four things?

  1. Is the committed launch date October 14 or October 18?
  2. Who is the final approver while Lena is out?
  3. What budget range should the team plan against?
  4. Which existing-client segment is in scope, and should prospects or other audiences be included?

I’ll treat the old deck as reference material and flag any positioning that needs re-approval. Once all four items are clear, I’ll finalize the kickoff brief.

Best,
Northstar delivery team

The point

Fluency is not reliability.

The valuable behavior is not writing a polished email. It is detecting contradictions, refusing to guess, and making the approval boundary visible.
