Where work leaks

Not every manual task deserves automation.

Start where repetition, delay, and dropped context already cost the team something. Then make the smallest dependable change.

Messy intake

Client requests arrive through email, forms, calls, and documents. Someone rebuilds the same brief by hand.

Follow-up drag

The team keeps asking for missing details, rewriting reminders, and wondering which request is stuck.

Status archaeology

A weekly report requires searching inboxes, sheets, project tools, and other people’s memory.

AI without brakes

A clever demo exists, but no one trusts it with live work because mistakes are invisible and actions are hard to review.

The 10-day sprint

One path from trigger to approved output.

Fixed scope prevents an “AI transformation” project from consuming your calendar. The result is a working process, not a slide deck. The 10-day clock starts after kickoff, approved access, and representative source material are ready.

01 / MAP

Choose the narrow lane

Define one trigger, the minimum inputs, the approved output, and the baseline we will measure.

02 / BUILD

Connect the boring parts

Use the tools your team already has where possible. Add extraction, drafting, routing, and tracking only where they earn their keep.

03 / BREAK

Test what goes wrong

Missing information, duplicates, unclear instructions, sensitive data, low confidence, and integration failures become explicit cases.

04 / HAND OFF

Put a human at the hinge

External messages and irreversible updates wait for review. You get logs, documentation, training, and a seven-day fix window.

Plain answers

Before anyone books a call.

Will this replace people on my team?

No. The sprint removes repetitive admin and drafting steps. A person reviews external communication and irreversible updates.

What tools do you use?

The smallest stack that fits: often Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, Sheets or Airtable, an existing CRM/project tool, and focused automation. Tool selection follows the workflow—not the other way around.

Do you need sensitive data?

The first design minimizes data and uses synthetic examples where possible. Sensitive access is requested only when necessary and explicitly approved. Regulated legal, medical, and financial decisions are out of scope.

What is not included?

Custom SaaS, CRM migrations, open-ended strategy, autonomous decisions, multiple workflows, and unlimited support. The sprint is intentionally narrow.

Why show a synthetic demo?

Because there are no customer stories to invent. The demo shows the intended mechanics honestly: structured output, missing-information flags, human approval, and an audit trail.
